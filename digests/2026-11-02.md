## K-12 Curriculum & Instruction

Recent news coverage in Chicago has focused on the upcoming election for the city's first elected school board, highlighting its significant implications for the district. Reports emphasize the substantial financial oversight the new board will hold, managing an estimated $10 billion budget, which underscores its broad impact on all city residents. Campaign activities are underway, with attention on candidates vying for positions, including the race for the elected school board president, featuring Jessica Biggs, Hilario Dominguez, and Victor Henderson.

The importance of scrutinizing campaign materials has also been noted, underscoring the need for informed voter participation as the city transitions to an elected board.

It is important to note that the provided articles primarily focus on the structure, election, and political implications of the new elected school board, rather than specific developments in K-12 curriculum and instruction.

## Students & School Life

Recent reports highlight both positive trends in student achievement and persistent challenges within Chicago Public Schools. The district announced an increase in its on-time high school graduation rate, exceeding 83% for 2026, alongside a rise in college enrollment rates. In efforts to support student well-being and community, CPS held its inaugural Black Student Union Conference and promoted a Youth Wellness Fair, indicating a focus on diverse student experiences and overall health.

However, significant concerns persist regarding special education services. Parents have reported instances of special education students being taught in hallways and in inadequate conditions, prompting questions about systemic gaps that may lead to students transferring between schools. Additionally, the district approved a financial settlement for a family whose child experienced years of racist bullying, underscoring ongoing issues related to student safety and school environment. The district also issued reminders about upcoming parent-teacher conferences and Election Day.

The effectiveness of the school board in addressing these ongoing concerns is a key point of discussion among stakeholders.

## Board of Education & Governance

The upcoming 2026 Chicago School Board election is a central focus, with extensive coverage on the candidates, their platforms, and the overall stakes. The citywide race for School Board President features candidates Jessica Biggs, Hilario Dominguez, and Victor Henderson, who participated in final forums and debates leading up to Election Day. Media outlets have provided voter guides and detailed analyses of the candidates' visions for Chicago Public Schools.

Key issues dominating the campaign discussions include strategies for balancing the CPS budget, particularly in light of the district's financial relationship with the state. Candidates also addressed concerns about special education gaps that contribute to student transfers, and some candidates have expressed interest in reintroducing school ratings, a practice Chicago previously discontinued. The election has seen significant financial investment, with millions of dollars pouring into the races from various sources, including substantial contributions from groups aligned with the Chicago Teachers Union, highlighting emerging campaign battlegrounds.

Beyond the election, a current school board member, Rivas, faced scrutiny over a 2018 work suspension related to past employment. The transition to an elected school board is also prompting discussions about the potential for increased political dynamics in local education governance.
