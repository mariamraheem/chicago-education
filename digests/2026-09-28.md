## K-12 Curriculum & Instruction

A recent opinion piece in the Chicago Tribune by Paige Ponder raises a timely question regarding K-12 curriculum and instruction: how should student success be defined as the city prepares for a fully elected school board? The article emphasizes the importance of establishing clear metrics and a shared understanding of what constitutes successful educational outcomes for Chicago Public Schools students.

This discussion is particularly relevant as the city transitions to a new governance structure, suggesting that the incoming school board will face the task of articulating and prioritizing specific goals for student achievement and development. The piece implicitly calls for stakeholders to consider various dimensions of success, which could influence future decisions regarding curriculum design, instructional strategies, and assessment methods across the district.

## Students & School Life

Chicago Public Schools (CPS) has reported a significant enrollment decline, losing over 11,000 students this year. This marks the largest drop since the pandemic and continues a decades-long downward trend for the district. Factors cited for the decrease include low birth rates and concerns among immigrant families. This substantial reduction in student numbers has prompted discussions about potential future school closures and the long-term implications for district funding.

Concurrently, discussions surrounding the upcoming fully elected Chicago school board have centered on the theme of student success. Candidates for the school board presidency, including Hilario Dominguez, Sendhil Revuluri, and Jessica Biggs, have emphasized their commitment to prioritizing student outcomes. Commentators have also raised questions about defining student success and whether the current board adequately centers it, indicating these issues will be prominent in the upcoming elections.
