## K-12 Curriculum & Instruction

Recent discussions in Chicago K-12 education highlight a dual focus on instructional priorities and the evolving landscape of school governance. Chicago Public Schools recently communicated a system-wide emphasis on fostering literacy for all students, indicating a continued focus on foundational reading and writing skills within the district's instructional framework.

This instructional focus coincides with broader conversations surrounding the upcoming transition to a fully elected school board. Stakeholders are beginning to discuss what constitutes "student success" in this new governance era. The composition and priorities of the incoming elected board are anticipated to influence how curriculum and instructional standards are set and how student achievement is defined and measured across Chicago Public Schools.

## Students & School Life

Chicago Public Schools continues to face a significant decline in student enrollment, with reports indicating a drop of 11,000 to 11,500 students this year. This decline is part of a decades-long trend, attributed by some sources to factors such as low birth rates and immigrant fears. The ongoing enrollment decrease has prompted discussions, including insights from former CPS CEO Dr. Janice Jackson, and raises questions about potential future school closures.

In other developments impacting student life, high school athletic events faced cancellations due to referees stopping work over alleged missed payments; however, this issue was reportedly resolved, allowing games to resume. Separately, parents at Prescott Elementary School expressed concerns regarding the displacement of special education students. Amid these discussions, the upcoming fully elected school board is a prominent theme, with candidates emphasizing "student success" and highlighting their experience as former students or teachers. This period also includes broader conversations about how to define and achieve student success within the district, alongside a message from Chicago Public Schools promoting literacy for all students.

A sustained decrease in student numbers typically has significant implications for the district's budget and financial planning. As state and federal funding often relies on per-pupil counts, a shrinking student body is likely to result in reduced overall revenue for CPS, potentially impacting resource allocation and school-level budgets in the coming years.

## Board of Education & Governance

The upcoming 2026 Chicago school board election, which will establish the city's first fully elected school board, is a central theme in recent discussions. A significant development in the race for Board President was the withdrawal of former Board Vice President Sendhil Revuluri, narrowing the field to three primary candidates. Voter guides and candidate profiles are being released to inform the public about the individuals running for both the presidential seat and various district positions.

The remaining candidates for Board President, Jessica Biggs and Hilario Dominguez, are both facing public scrutiny regarding their pasts. The CPS watchdog has reopened a case concerning misconduct allegations against Hilario Dominguez, who has publicly responded to these claims. Similarly, Jessica Biggs' 2018 firing as a CPS principal has been a point of discussion. The broader election is being characterized as a significant moment for the future direction of Chicago Public Schools, with some parents expressing concern over the presence of a political group at a school event as the election draws nearer.

## Labor & Unions

The upcoming transition to a fully elected Chicago school board represents a significant shift in the governance of Chicago Public Schools. This new board will be tasked with defining core educational priorities, including what constitutes "student success." This development is particularly relevant to labor and unions, as the elected board will be the primary body responsible for negotiating contracts with the Chicago Teachers Union (CTU) and other school employee unions, and for setting policies that impact working conditions and staffing levels.

The discussion around defining student success, as highlighted by recent commentary, underscores a critical area where various stakeholders, including labor organizations, will likely seek to influence the new board's agenda. Unions often advocate for specific educational approaches, resource allocations, and staffing models that they believe best serve students while also aligning with their members' professional expertise and working environments. The priorities established by the elected board regarding student outcomes will therefore directly inform future labor relations and potential points of collaboration or tension.
